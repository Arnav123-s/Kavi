## Selection

Prefer dated notebooks, diaries, drafts and correspondence by scientists and philosophers. Keep the author's own language and the order of entries. Published arguments and retrospective memoirs can supplement that material, but belong to different source categories. A published first-person account is a record of what an author chose to express; it is not a direct recording of every thought.

## First-person accounts involving psychiatric diagnoses

The selection criterion is a voluntarily published account with explicit provenance. A diagnosis is never inferred from the vocabulary, argument, handwriting or subject matter. The precise label and the source reporting it are recorded separately. No attempt is made to identify pseudonymous authors or obtain private clinical files.

No comparably verified original diary by an author with a documented psychopathy assessment was established in this search. A book title, brain scan or self-description is insufficient to fill that gap. The labels used by different authors and historical clinicians are not silently treated as interchangeable.

These accounts may supply useful examples of how a person describes perception, doubt, conviction, another person's objection and later reconsideration. Whether they improve Kavi is unmeasured. Diagnosis does not make an account more accurate, articulate or intellectually valuable. The same source-quality and evaluation criteria apply to every author.

## Teaching objective

The intended skill is to construct a changing interpretation of a passage: who holds a belief, what they claim, what evidence they offer, what remains uncertain and what a later statement changes. These distinctions must be learned and tested. Storing passages and finding a similar sentence would not demonstrate the intended capability.

An account can accurately report that its author experienced or believed something without establishing that the described external event occurred. The configuration must eventually distinguish attribution from endorsement. Diagnosis metadata belongs to the external source catalogue; it is not an answer key or a shortcut for judging an author's claims.

The existing direct event model chooses an arithmetic operation from two-quantity questions. Its output space cannot express the argument structures required here. The adaptive extension changes executable connections during input, but still uses that narrow output space. Passing prose through it now would not constitute teaching philosophy or understanding experience. The [current results](../experiments/2026-09-07-incremental-inquiry-transfer.md) record this boundary.

## Proposed sequence and checks

1. **Establish a usable edition.** Record author, date, archive identifier, language, editor, translator, access conditions and a fingerprint of each admitted local file. Keep manuscript text, editorial additions and uncertain transcription spans distinguishable. A publicly readable page is not by itself a licence to republish a book or its modern translation.
2. **Define a learnable relationship.** Extend the learner's output vocabulary to represent an assertion, its holder, its supporting relation, scope and revision. These are task interfaces, not claims that named mental states have already emerged. Compare a supplied interface against roles induced from correction.
3. **Use authentic evidence.** Use the original passage and its actual later revision, published question, objection or reply where available. Do not invent diary entries, paraphrased teaching examples, altered facts, solutions or simulated patient narratives. If suitable supervision is absent, keep that task untrained instead of fabricating it.
4. **Correct a reusable distinction.** Check a prediction before revealing the original continuation or correction. Record whether the update changes a general connection, creates a new one, or merely encodes a particular passage. Test earlier valid relationships after the change.
5. **Separate examination.** Reserve complete works or authors before model selection. Do not place translations or adjacent excerpts from the same passage in different partitions. Use genuine independently published questions and answers where they assess the same skill; access to those final answers is restricted to the evaluator.
6. **Measure benefit and cost.** Compare the same configuration before and after teaching, with the same inference budget. Count unassisted correctness, response coverage, unsupported assertions, attribution errors, useful questions, old-skill regressions, persistent bytes, temporary activity, teacher storage and all search work. An increase in output frequency alone is not a learning improvement.

## Current disposition

Ten sources are catalogued. None has been admitted as a new teaching corpus, copied into an inference artifact or used in the five measured English courses. Modern memoirs remain reference candidates until an appropriate usable edition is available. The next engineering requirement is a learner that can represent scope and attributed claims; the catalogue does not resolve that requirement or establish research-level understanding.
